# Enneagram Demo LMS — Foundation Content, Structural Model, and Six Demo Personas

**Status:** Working content foundation  
**Purpose:** Populate Parts 1–3 of the agreed demo-content plan before designing the skills/course catalogue.  
**Scope:** Nine core types; Three Centers; lines of connection; concise Nine Levels of Development; six deliberately designed fictional team members.

> **Demo-model note:** The six personas use a custom **weighted top-three Type Profile**. This is not presented as formal Trifix/Tritype methodology. The primary type remains the main Enneagram pattern; the secondary and tertiary scores are used as demo signals that add nuance and later help weight learning recommendations.

# Part 1 — Nine-Type Foundation

## Type 1 — The Reformer / Improver

### Primary description
Type 1 is oriented toward improvement, integrity, correctness and responsible action. Ones tend to notice where reality falls short of a standard and feel an internal pull to correct, refine or improve it. At their best, this produces principled judgement, reliability, discernment and a genuine commitment to making systems and situations better. Under strain, the same orientation can harden into perfectionism, criticism, rigidity and frustration with themselves or others.

### Core motivation / orientation
To act with integrity, be responsible, improve what is imperfect, and avoid being wrong, corrupt or defective.

### Primary traits
- principled;
- conscientious;
- organised;
- discerning;
- self-controlled;
- improvement-oriented.

### Primary strengths
- high standards and quality awareness;
- responsibility and dependability;
- ethical judgement;
- attention to errors and inconsistencies;
- disciplined follow-through;
- willingness to improve systems.

### Primary challenges
- perfectionism;
- excessive self-criticism;
- impatience with mistakes;
- difficulty accepting “good enough”;
- rigidity around preferred methods;
- frustration when others appear careless.

### Typical blind spots
- believing criticism is simply objective correction;
- underestimating how demanding their standards feel to others;
- treating personal preferences as universal standards;
- failing to notice accumulated resentment;
- overlooking experimentation because the correct method seems obvious.

### Common stressors
- poor-quality work;
- unclear standards;
- preventable mistakes;
- perceived irresponsibility;
- being required to compromise important principles;
- environments that feel disorderly or careless.

### Growth themes
Flexibility, acceptance, constructive feedback, delegation, experimentation, self-compassion and distinguishing essential standards from preferences.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Standards and discernment → perfectionism and correction → rigidity / criticism → flexible judgement and constructive improvement**

## Type 2 — The Helper / Supporter

### Primary description
Type 2 is oriented toward connection, support and responding to other people's needs. Twos often notice what would help another person and can create warmth, encouragement and strong interpersonal bonds. At their best, they give generously without losing sight of themselves. Under strain, helpfulness can become over-involvement, people-pleasing or an indirect attempt to secure appreciation and closeness.

### Core motivation / orientation
To feel loved, valued and connected by being caring, useful and responsive to others.

### Primary traits
- warm;
- empathetic;
- encouraging;
- relational;
- generous;
- attentive to others.

### Primary strengths
- interpersonal awareness;
- empathy;
- encouragement;
- relationship building;
- recognising others' needs;
- creating supportive environments.

### Primary challenges
- weak boundaries;
- difficulty identifying their own needs;
- over-helping;
- seeking approval indirectly;
- difficulty saying no;
- becoming indispensable rather than enabling independence.

### Typical blind spots
- assuming help is wanted because it is well-intentioned;
- overlooking their own expectations of reciprocity;
- seeing self-sacrifice as purely generous;
- underestimating the pressure created by unsolicited support;
- mistaking being needed for genuine closeness.

### Common stressors
- feeling unappreciated;
- rejection;
- relational distance;
- being unable to help;
- having their contribution overlooked;
- needing to prioritise themselves over others.

### Growth themes
Boundaries, self-awareness, direct requests, receiving as well as giving, assertiveness and sustainable support.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Empathy and support → over-helping → blurred boundaries / resentment → sustainable support and self-advocacy**

## Type 3 — The Achiever / Performer

### Primary description
Type 3 is oriented toward achievement, effectiveness and demonstrating value through accomplishment. Threes often adapt quickly to expectations, organise themselves around goals and know how to turn effort into visible results. At their best, they combine ambition with authenticity and inspire others through effective action. Under strain, achievement can become image management, overwork, comparison or disconnection from personal feelings and values.

### Core motivation / orientation
To feel valuable and worthwhile through achievement, competence and recognised contribution.

### Primary traits
- driven;
- adaptable;
- energetic;
- pragmatic;
- goal-oriented;
- performance-conscious.

### Primary strengths
- execution;
- goal focus;
- adaptability;
- motivation;
- efficiency;
- ability to mobilise others around results.

### Primary challenges
- over-identification with achievement;
- workaholism;
- impatience with slower processes;
- excessive comparison;
- image management;
- losing touch with what they genuinely want.

### Typical blind spots
- assuming visible results tell the whole story;
- underestimating the relational cost of constant performance pressure;
- adapting so effectively that personal preferences become unclear;
- treating vulnerability as inefficiency;
- confusing external recognition with internal value.

### Common stressors
- public failure;
- loss of status;
- stalled progress;
- unclear success criteria;
- appearing incompetent;
- environments in which effort is not recognised.

### Growth themes
Authenticity, emotional literacy, sustainable performance, listening, collaboration and separating self-worth from output.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Achievement and adaptability → relentless performance → image focus / disconnection → authentic and sustainable effectiveness**

## Type 4 — The Individualist / Creator

### Primary description
Type 4 is oriented toward identity, meaning, emotional depth and authentic self-expression. Fours often perceive nuance, emotional texture and what makes a person, idea or experience distinctive. At their best, this produces creativity, sensitivity, insight and an ability to transform experience into something meaningful. Under strain, depth can become self-absorption, comparison, mood dependence or difficulty acting when reality feels ordinary or emotionally unsatisfying.

### Core motivation / orientation
To understand and express an authentic identity and to experience life as meaningful and personally significant.

### Primary traits
- expressive;
- introspective;
- sensitive;
- imaginative;
- individualistic;
- emotionally perceptive.

### Primary strengths
- creativity;
- emotional insight;
- authenticity;
- aesthetic sensitivity;
- empathy for complexity;
- ability to find meaning in experience.

### Primary challenges
- comparison;
- mood-dependent action;
- emotional over-identification;
- idealising what is absent;
- inconsistency in execution;
- feeling misunderstood or fundamentally different.

### Typical blind spots
- assuming strong feeling necessarily means accurate interpretation;
- overlooking ordinary solutions because they lack emotional resonance;
- treating emotional intensity as evidence of authenticity;
- underestimating how withdrawal affects colleagues;
- comparing internal struggles with others' external presentation.

### Common stressors
- feeling unseen or misunderstood;
- emotionally flat environments;
- lack of creative autonomy;
- perceived inauthenticity;
- comparison with others;
- routine without visible meaning.

### Growth themes
Grounded action, emotional regulation, consistency, perspective-taking, practical execution and appreciating what is already present.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Depth and authenticity → emotional absorption → withdrawal / inconsistency → grounded self-expression and action**

## Type 5 — The Investigator / Observer

### Primary description
Type 5 is oriented toward understanding, competence, autonomy and conserving personal resources. Fives often step back to observe, analyse and build mastery before engaging. At their best, this creates penetrating insight, expertise, originality and independent thinking. Under strain, thoughtful distance can become excessive detachment, information gathering without action, guardedness or isolation.

### Core motivation / orientation
To be capable and competent by understanding the environment and maintaining sufficient knowledge, autonomy and internal resources.

### Primary traits
- analytical;
- perceptive;
- curious;
- independent;
- focused;
- private.

### Primary strengths
- deep analysis;
- specialist expertise;
- concentration;
- independent thinking;
- conceptual clarity;
- ability to investigate complex problems.

### Primary challenges
- over-analysis;
- delayed engagement;
- limited communication;
- excessive privacy;
- conserving time or energy too rigidly;
- substituting understanding for participation.

### Typical blind spots
- believing more information is always required before action;
- underestimating how silence can be interpreted by others;
- treating emotional or relational demands primarily as resource costs;
- failing to share useful thinking early enough;
- mistaking detachment for objectivity.

### Common stressors
- intrusion;
- excessive demands;
- insufficient preparation time;
- being expected to respond before understanding;
- emotionally intense environments;
- loss of privacy or autonomy.

### Growth themes
Engagement, decisive action, knowledge sharing, embodied presence, collaboration and communicating before analysis feels complete.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Analysis and independence → detachment and endless preparation → isolation / delayed action → engaged expertise and decisive participation**

## Type 6 — The Loyalist / Guardian

### Primary description
Type 6 is oriented toward security, reliability, preparedness and identifying what can be trusted. Sixes often scan for risks, test assumptions and consider what could go wrong before committing. At their best, this produces courage, loyalty, foresight and exceptional troubleshooting. Under strain, responsible vigilance can become self-doubt, suspicion, indecision or escalating worst-case scenarios.

### Core motivation / orientation
To find security, trustworthy guidance and dependable support while being prepared for uncertainty and risk.

### Primary traits
- responsible;
- vigilant;
- loyal;
- questioning;
- prepared;
- collaborative.

### Primary strengths
- risk awareness;
- troubleshooting;
- loyalty;
- preparation;
- perseverance;
- identifying weaknesses in plans.

### Primary challenges
- self-doubt;
- indecision;
- over-checking;
- anxiety about uncertain outcomes;
- suspicion;
- difficulty trusting their own judgement.

### Typical blind spots
- interpreting repeated checking as purely responsible;
- failing to notice when risk analysis has stopped adding value;
- projecting uncertainty onto other people's motives;
- seeking reassurance while simultaneously distrusting it;
- treating unlikely possibilities as if they deserve equal weight.

### Common stressors
- ambiguity;
- inconsistent leadership;
- unreliable commitments;
- sudden changes;
- unclear authority;
- being forced to decide without enough certainty.

### Growth themes
Decision confidence, calibrated risk, ambiguity tolerance, self-trust, proportionate preparation and distinguishing useful caution from fear-driven checking.

### Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development
**Risk awareness and preparedness → hyper-vigilance → doubt / paralysis → calibrated judgement and courageous action**

At their best, they combine curiosity with depth and turn diverse interests into productive creativity. Under strain, possibility seeking can become scattering, impulsivity, avoidance of discomfort or difficulty following through.

### Core motivation / orientation

**Possibility and enthusiasm → constant expansion → scattering / unfinished work → focused creativity and completion**

# Part 2 — Structural Enneagram Layer

# 2A. The Three Centers

## Body / Instinctive Center — Types 8, 9 and 1

The Body Center is concerned broadly with action, autonomy, boundaries, instinctive responses and how a person meets the physical and social environment. Its strengths include grounded action, presence and practical engagement. Its difficulties can involve control, resistance, anger, rigidity or disengagement.

- **8** tends to express instinctive energy directly and forcefully.
- **9** tends to soften, diffuse or suppress it in the interest of stability.
- **1** tends to regulate and channel it through standards and self-control.

## Heart / Feeling Center — Types 2, 3 and 4

The Heart Center is concerned broadly with relationship, identity, value, emotional information and how the self is experienced in relation to other people. Its strengths include empathy, emotional intelligence, motivation and self-expression. Its difficulties can involve image, comparison, approval and over-identification with interpersonal feedback.

- **2** focuses strongly on others' needs and relational connection.
- **3** focuses on value, achievement and the image of effectiveness.
- **4** focuses on personal identity, emotional meaning and authenticity.

## Head / Thinking Center — Types 5, 6 and 7

The Head Center is concerned broadly with understanding, anticipation, uncertainty, planning and making sense of what may happen. Its strengths include analysis, imagination, foresight and preparation. Its difficulties can involve overthinking, anxiety, mental escape or disconnection from immediate experience.

- **5** seeks understanding and mastery through observation.
- **6** anticipates risk and tests what can be trusted.
- **7** generates possibilities and options to preserve freedom and satisfaction.

# 2B. Lines of Connection

For the demo, lines are treated as **dynamic resources and risks**, not as a simplistic rule that one connected type is always “good” and the other always “bad.” The classic Riso–Hudson stress/growth directions are retained because they are useful content, while the learning interpretation allows constructive qualities to be drawn from either connected point.

| Primary | Classic growth direction | Growth resources | Classic stress direction | Reactive pattern to notice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 7 | spontaneity, openness, experimentation, enjoyment | 4 | moodiness, self-absorption, feeling misunderstood |
| **2** | 4 | self-awareness, emotional honesty, attention to own needs | 8 | forcefulness, control, confrontation when unappreciated |
| **3** | 6 | cooperation, loyalty, shared purpose, commitment | 9 | disengagement, inertia, loss of momentum |
| **4** | 1 | structure, objectivity, discipline, principled action | 2 | approval-seeking, over-involvement, interpersonal dependency |
| **5** | 8 | decisiveness, confidence, embodied action, presence | 7 | scattering, hyperactivity, jumping among possibilities |
| **6** | 9 | calm, trust, steadiness, broader perspective | 3 | competitive proving, image concern, over-performance |
| **7** | 5 | depth, concentration, analysis, restraint | 1 | criticism, rigidity, frustration with imperfection |
| **8** | 2 | empathy, generosity, relational openness, care | 5 | withdrawal, secrecy, excessive detachment |
| **9** | 3 | agency, goals, purposeful action, visible engagement | 6 | worry, doubt, vigilance, indecision |

## Connection interpretation by type

### Type 1
**Toward 7:** The Improver can gain flexibility by allowing experimentation, enjoyment and alternative routes rather than assuming improvement requires tighter control.  
**Toward 4:** Under strain, disappointment can become personalised, emotionally charged or focused on what is missing. The useful lesson from 4 is emotional awareness without abandoning objective judgement.

### Type 2
**Toward 4:** Growth involves turning some of the attention normally directed toward others inward: recognising personal feelings, identity and needs.  
**Toward 8:** Under strain, indirect helping can turn into forceful demands or control. The constructive 8 resource is directness: asking openly rather than manipulating indirectly.

### Type 3
**Toward 6:** Growth broadens achievement from individual performance toward loyalty, teamwork, shared purpose and dependable contribution.  
**Toward 9:** Under strain, relentless activity can collapse into disengagement. The constructive 9 resource is the ability to slow down without losing purposeful engagement.

### Type 4
**Toward 1:** Growth brings structure, objectivity, discipline and the ability to act according to commitments rather than waiting for the right emotional state.  
**Toward 2:** Under strain, the search for connection can become approval-seeking or over-involvement. The constructive 2 resource is genuine outward attention and contribution.

### Type 5
**Toward 8:** Growth converts understanding into confident participation, action and a willingness to occupy space.  
**Toward 7:** Under strain, concentrated thinking can fragment into scattered ideas and nervous activity. The constructive 7 resource is curiosity and possibility without abandoning depth.

### Type 6
**Toward 9:** Growth introduces calm, trust, perspective and the ability to remain steady without resolving every uncertainty first.  
**Toward 3:** Under strain, insecurity can become proving, over-performance or competitive comparison. The constructive 3 resource is confident action and goal focus.

### Type 7
**Toward 5:** Growth turns breadth into depth: sustained attention, investigation and the ability to stay with one experience.  
**Toward 1:** Under strain, flexibility can become frustrated criticism and insistence on how things should be. The constructive 1 resource is discipline and standards.

### Type 8
**Toward 2:** Growth opens strength toward care, generosity, empathy and visible concern for others.  
**Toward 5:** Under strain, forceful engagement can reverse into withdrawal and guarded strategising. The constructive 5 resource is thoughtful observation before action.

### Type 9
**Toward 3:** Growth activates agency, goals, momentum and willingness to make personal priorities visible.  
**Toward 6:** Under strain, apparent calm can give way to worry, doubt and threat scanning. The constructive 6 resource is realistic preparation and responsible questioning.

# 2C. Nine Levels of Development

## How the demo will use the Levels

The Riso–Hudson model contains nine Levels within every type:

- **Levels 1–3:** Healthy / increasingly free and constructive expression
- **Levels 4–6:** Average / increasingly habitual and compensatory expression
- **Levels 7–9:** Unhealthy / increasingly rigid and dysfunctional expression

The following entries are **concise demo adaptations**, not reproductions of the source descriptions. They are designed for cards, vertical pathways and later development interfaces.

The demo should not imply that course completion automatically moves a learner between psychological levels.

---

## Type 1 Levels — Reformer / Improver

### Level 1 — Wise Discernment
Standards become flexible wisdom rather than rigid rules. The person sees reality clearly and chooses what genuinely improves the situation.  
**Observable signs:** perspective, acceptance, humane judgement, calm discernment.

### Level 2 — Principled Self-Discipline
Strong convictions are balanced by maturity and reason. Standards guide behaviour without requiring constant correction of others.  
**Observable signs:** responsibility, moderation, integrity, consistency.

### Level 3 — Ethical Contribution
Principles are translated into useful service, fairness and dependable action.  
**Observable signs:** fairness, conscientiousness, teaching by example, purposeful improvement.

### Level 4 — Compelled to Improve
Attention increasingly fixes on what should be better. Improvement begins to feel personally necessary.  
**Observable signs:** frequent correction, dissatisfaction, advocacy, “ought” and “should” language.

### Level 5 — Controlled Perfectionism
Fear of error increases reliance on order, rules and self-control.  
**Observable signs:** rigidity, overwork, emotional restraint, excessive attention to procedure.

### Level 6 — Critical Enforcement
Standards harden into judgement. Mistakes become difficult to tolerate.  
**Observable signs:** impatience, moralising, fault-finding, abrasive correction.

### Level 7 — Rigid Certainty
Alternative viewpoints are increasingly experienced as wrong rather than merely different.  
**Observable signs:** dogmatism, inflexibility, severe judgement.

### Level 8 — Obsession with Wrongness
Attention becomes dominated by imperfection and wrongdoing, while contradictions in the person's own behaviour may be rationalised.  
**Observable signs:** obsessive correction, hypocrisy, fixation on faults.

### Level 9 — Punitive Destructiveness
The drive to eliminate what is perceived as wrong becomes extreme and destructive.  
**Observable signs:** condemnation, punitive behaviour, severe psychological distress.

## Type 2 Levels — Helper / Supporter

### Level 1 — Unconditional Care
Care is freely offered without requiring dependence, gratitude or repayment. Self and others are valued equally.  
**Observable signs:** humility, generosity, self-care, non-possessive support.

### Level 2 — Empathic Connection
Warmth and emotional awareness create genuine, compassionate relationships.  
**Observable signs:** sincerity, forgiveness, attentiveness, emotional generosity.

### Level 3 — Sustainable Support
Helping remains generous while personal needs and limits are recognised.  
**Observable signs:** encouragement, appreciation, healthy giving, appropriate boundaries.

### Level 4 — Approval-Seeking Helpfulness
Connection increasingly depends on being liked, useful or appreciated.  
**Observable signs:** people-pleasing, flattery, excessive availability.

### Level 5 — Needed to Be Needed
Support becomes intrusive or creates dependence, often with unspoken expectations in return.  
**Observable signs:** hovering, meddling, possessiveness, difficulty stepping back.

### Level 6 — Indispensable Helper
The person increasingly assumes that others need their involvement and may emphasise their sacrifices.  
**Observable signs:** martyrdom, self-importance, patronising help.

### Level 7 — Manipulated Reciprocity
Unmet expectations can turn into guilt, indirect pressure or aggressive attempts to secure recognition.  
**Observable signs:** guilt-inducing behaviour, resentment, concealed self-interest.

### Level 8 — Coercive Entitlement
The belief that past giving creates a right to repayment becomes increasingly forceful.  
**Observable signs:** domination, coercion, demands.

### Level 9 — Embittered Victimhood
Resentment and self-deception become pervasive, with responsibility increasingly projected outward.  
**Observable signs:** chronic grievance, blame, severe relational dysfunction.

---

## Type 3 Levels — Achiever / Performer

### Level 1 — Authentic Value
Achievement is no longer required to prove worth. Capability and ambition are expressed from a secure sense of self.  
**Observable signs:** authenticity, humility, generosity, inner direction.

### Level 2 — Confident Capability
Healthy self-belief fuels energetic and adaptable performance.  
**Observable signs:** competence, confidence, charm, constructive ambition.

### Level 3 — Inspiring Excellence
Achievement becomes a visible example of disciplined self-development that can motivate others.  
**Observable signs:** effectiveness, aspiration, positive role modelling.

### Level 4 — Performance Equals Worth
Success becomes increasingly tied to self-esteem and comparison.  
**Observable signs:** relentless goals, status awareness, fear of failure.

### Level 5 — Image Management
Attention shifts from doing valuable work toward ensuring that success is seen and recognised.  
**Observable signs:** packaging, impression management, emotional disconnection.

### Level 6 — Competitive Superiority
The need to appear exceptional intensifies.  
**Observable signs:** self-promotion, arrogance, exaggerated claims, contemptuous comparison.

### Level 7 — Success at Any Cost
Fear of exposure or failure can override ordinary relational or ethical restraints.  
**Observable signs:** opportunism, exploitation, intense envy.

### Level 8 — Deceptive Preservation
Protecting the successful image becomes more important than truth or trust.  
**Observable signs:** deception, sabotage, betrayal.

### Level 9 — Destructive Comparison
Other people's success becomes intolerable because it exposes an unbearable sense of personal failure.  
**Observable signs:** vindictiveness, obsessive rivalry, severe dysfunction.

---

## Type 4 Levels — Individualist / Creator

### Level 1 — Transformative Creativity
Personal experience becomes material for creative renewal rather than a fixed identity.  
**Observable signs:** inspiration, originality, resilience, generative self-expression.

### Level 2 — Deep Self-Awareness
Feelings are recognised clearly without completely defining the self.  
**Observable signs:** intuition, sensitivity, compassion, emotional insight.

### Level 3 — Authentic Expression
Individuality is expressed openly and honestly while retaining emotional strength.  
**Observable signs:** candour, humanity, self-expression, balanced vulnerability.

### Level 4 — Romantic Intensification
The person increasingly cultivates emotional atmosphere and idealised experience.  
**Observable signs:** fantasy, aesthetic preoccupation, emotional amplification.

### Level 5 — Interior Absorption
Experience is increasingly taken personally and processed inwardly.  
**Observable signs:** hypersensitivity, withdrawal, self-consciousness, moodiness.

### Level 6 — Identity Through Difference
Being different becomes central to identity and practical engagement declines.  
**Observable signs:** envy, self-indulgence, impracticality, disdain, fantasy.

### Level 7 — Blocked Despair
Failed ideals turn inward and inhibit action.  
**Observable signs:** shame, alienation, paralysis, severe discouragement.

### Level 8 — Self-Condemnation
Internal criticism and painful self-interpretation become overwhelming.  
**Observable signs:** self-hatred, torment, rejection of support.

### Level 9 — Destructive Hopelessness
The person loses meaningful connection to possibility and may become severely self-destructive.  
**Observable signs:** profound despair, breakdown, dangerous withdrawal.

---

## Type 5 Levels — Investigator / Observer

### Level 1 — Visionary Understanding
Knowledge is integrated into broad, open-minded insight that can transform how problems are understood.  
**Observable signs:** originality, synthesis, contextual thinking, pioneering insight.

### Level 2 — Penetrating Perception
Curiosity and concentration are exceptionally strong while remaining connected to reality.  
**Observable signs:** observation, foresight, sustained attention, intellectual openness.

### Level 3 — Expert Mastery
Knowledge becomes practical mastery and original contribution.  
**Observable signs:** expertise, invention, independence, productive specialisation.

### Level 4 — Preparation Before Participation
The person increasingly feels the need to understand, model or prepare before acting.  
**Observable signs:** research, rehearsal, specialisation, delayed engagement.

### Level 5 — Detached Conceptualisation
Internal models become more compelling than direct participation.  
**Observable signs:** withdrawal, abstraction, preoccupation, reduced practical engagement.

### Level 6 — Defensive Intellectualisation
Ideas become a protected territory and disagreement can feel intrusive.  
**Observable signs:** cynicism, provocation, argumentativeness, extreme positions.

### Level 7 — Isolated Rejection
Withdrawal becomes severe and connection with ordinary social reality deteriorates.  
**Observable signs:** reclusion, nihilism, intense defensiveness.

### Level 8 — Frightening Mental Fixation
Thought becomes increasingly obsessive and distorted rather than clarifying.  
**Observable signs:** fixation, fear, distorted interpretations.

### Level 9 — Breakdown of Reality Contact
Isolation and destructive mental processes become extreme.  
**Observable signs:** severe disorganisation, self-destructive behaviour, psychological breakdown.

---

## Type 6 Levels — Loyalist / Guardian

### Level 1 — Courageous Self-Trust
Security increasingly comes from internal confidence rather than certainty from outside.  
**Observable signs:** courage, independence, cooperation, positive leadership.

### Level 2 — Trusting Attachment
Strong relationships are built through genuine trust and reciprocal commitment.  
**Observable signs:** warmth, loyalty, affection, dependable bonds.

### Level 3 — Responsible Community Building
Preparedness and loyalty become stabilising contributions to groups and causes.  
**Observable signs:** reliability, perseverance, teamwork, responsible planning.

### Level 4 — Security Through Structure
The person increasingly invests in systems, authorities, plans and alliances that promise stability.  
**Observable signs:** vigilance, contingency planning, rule awareness, checking.

### Level 5 — Anxious Ambivalence
Internal uncertainty makes action difficult and responses increasingly contradictory.  
**Observable signs:** indecision, procrastination, mixed signals, reactivity.

### Level 6 — Defensive Threat Response
Fear is increasingly managed by locating danger outside the self.  
**Observable signs:** suspicion, blame, hostility, us-versus-them thinking.

### Level 7 — Panicked Dependence
Confidence collapses and the person feels unable to manage threats independently.  
**Observable signs:** panic, self-disparagement, desperate search for certainty.

### Level 8 — Persecuted Reactivity
Threat interpretation becomes increasingly detached from proportionate evidence.  
**Observable signs:** irrational lashing out, extreme suspicion, escalating fear.

### Level 9 — Self-Destructive Escape
Fear and perceived threat become psychologically overwhelming.  
**Observable signs:** severe instability, self-destructive behaviour, breakdown.

## Type 7 Levels — Enthusiast / Explorer

### Level 1 — Deep Appreciation
Experience is absorbed fully rather than chased. Satisfaction comes from presence rather than accumulation.  
**Observable signs:** gratitude, depth, wonder, contentment.

### Level 2 — Vital Engagement
Energy and curiosity remain spontaneous without becoming scattered.  
**Observable signs:** enthusiasm, resilience, responsiveness, joy.

### Level 3 — Productive Versatility
Multiple interests become real capability and useful cross-disciplinary contribution.  
**Observable signs:** productivity, versatility, creativity, practical accomplishment.

### Level 4 — Expanding Options
Restlessness increases the need for choices, novelty and stimulation.  
**Observable signs:** option-seeking, novelty, reduced focus, constant planning.

### Level 5 — Hyperactive Possibility
The person has difficulty refusing impulses or staying with one commitment.  
**Observable signs:** over-scheduling, unfinished ideas, constant activity, weak follow-through.

### Level 6 — Excess and Dissatisfaction
More stimulation produces less satisfaction, encouraging further consumption and demand.  
**Observable signs:** excess, impatience, materialism, insensitivity.

### Level 7 — Impulsive Escape
Activity becomes an urgent attempt to avoid anxiety or discomfort.  
**Observable signs:** impulsivity, escalating excess, poor restraint.

### Level 8 — Compulsive Flight
The person increasingly acts on impulses rather than tolerating difficult internal experience.  
**Observable signs:** erratic behaviour, mood swings, loss of control.

### Level 9 — Exhausted Despair
The strategy of continual escape collapses as energy and hope are depleted.  
**Observable signs:** panic, severe exhaustion, despair, self-destructive behaviour.

## Type 8 Levels — Challenger / Protector

### Level 1 — Magnanimous Strength
Power is governed by restraint, purpose and concern for something larger than the self.  
**Observable signs:** courage, mercy, self-restraint, service-oriented authority.

### Level 2 — Grounded Self-Confidence
Strength is secure enough that it does not need constant demonstration.  
**Observable signs:** confidence, resourcefulness, assertiveness, initiative.

### Level 3 — Protective Leadership
Decisiveness is used to mobilise and protect others.  
**Observable signs:** leadership, honour, advocacy, decisive support.

### Level 4 — Rugged Self-Sufficiency
Independence and control of resources become increasingly important.  
**Observable signs:** risk-taking, hard work, emotional self-denial, strong autonomy.

### Level 5 — Environmental Domination
The person increasingly needs others and circumstances to align with their will.  
**Observable signs:** forcefulness, command, ego expansion, reduced equality.

### Level 6 — Adversarial Control
Disagreement becomes a contest of wills and intimidation replaces influence.  
**Observable signs:** combativeness, threats, escalation, refusal to yield.

### Level 7 — Ruthless Defiance
Resistance to control becomes extreme and ordinary limits lose authority.  
**Observable signs:** ruthlessness, dictatorial behaviour, destructive defiance.

### Level 8 — Invulnerability Fantasy
The person increasingly overestimates their power and ability to prevail.  
**Observable signs:** recklessness, grandiosity, extreme overextension.

### Level 9 — Destructive Retaliation
The need not to be defeated becomes more important than preserving relationships or systems.  
**Observable signs:** vengeance, destructive aggression, severe dysfunction.

## Type 9 Levels — Peacemaker / Harmoniser

### Level 1 — Fully Present Harmony
Inner stability no longer requires self-erasure. The person is strongly connected both to self and others.  
**Observable signs:** autonomy, vitality, deep connection, equanimity.

### Level 2 — Serene Receptivity
Acceptance and calm arise from genuine stability rather than avoidance.  
**Observable signs:** patience, trust, emotional steadiness, openness.

### Level 3 — Active Harmonising
The person uses receptivity actively to connect people and integrate perspectives.  
**Observable signs:** mediation, synthesis, reassurance, engaged communication.

### Level 4 — Accommodating Peacekeeping
Conflict avoidance begins to override personal preferences.  
**Observable signs:** automatic agreement, self-effacement, reluctance to state needs.

The person stays active enough to function but increasingly tunes out problems that could disturb equilibrium.  
**Observable signs:** distraction, complacency, hazy priorities, emotional disengagement.

### Level 6 — Peace at Any Price
Problems are minimised and change feels increasingly futile or disruptive.  
**Observable signs:** procrastination, stubborn passivity, resignation, appeasement.

### Level 7 — Repressed Withdrawal
The person increasingly disconnects from conflict and from their own capacity to respond.  
**Observable signs:** obstinacy, ineffectiveness, severe avoidance.

### Level 8 — Numbing Disconnection
Avoidance becomes profound disconnection from experience and effective functioning.  
**Observable signs:** numbness, dissociation, inability to engage.

### Level 9 — Severe Abandonment of Self
Connection with personal agency and ordinary functioning becomes profoundly disrupted.  
**Observable signs:** severe disorientation, collapse, psychological breakdown.

# Part 3 — Six Deliberately Designed Demo Personas

## Why these six profiles are designed rather than random

The demo personas should collectively demonstrate the capabilities of the platform.

The six profiles below intentionally include:

1. **two people with the same primary Type 9 but very different secondary patterns;**
2. **one top-three profile spanning all three Centers;**
3. **one strongly Head-dominant profile;**
4. **a natural high-friction pairing;**
5. **a highly complementary pairing;**
6. **a secondary influence that reinforces a primary challenge;**
7. **a secondary influence that moderates a primary challenge;**
8. a mixture of leadership, specialist, operational and interpersonal roles.

The scores are fictional assessment results created for the demo.

## 1. Emily Carter — Client Success & Project Lead

### Likely stress pattern
As pressure rises, Type 9 disengagement can combine with Type 6 doubt: she may delay a difficult decision, seek more reassurance and become increasingly preoccupied with possible interpersonal consequences.

### Strongest development opportunities
- healthy boundaries;
- assertive communication;
- decision confidence;
- constructive conflict;
- facilitative leadership.

### Why this persona is useful in the demo
Emily provides a strong example of **converging recommendations across three different type signals**.

# Recommended Next Phase

The next phase should begin from the **actual six personas**, not from the entire Enneagram universe.

The useful question is now:

> **Across these six people, what strengths and development needs recur often enough to justify skills and courses in the demo LMS?**

That analysis can identify a focused skills set and course catalogue whose recommendations feel personalised while each canonical course remains assigned to one formal pathway.

A likely next workflow is:

1. extract the development themes across all six personas;
2. consolidate overlapping themes into a small skills taxonomy;
3. identify which skills represent strengthening strengths versus fortifying growth areas;
4. identify stress-management and integration skills;
5. determine which skills are useful for team interaction;
6. create only the courses required to make those pathways convincing;
7. then map those courses back onto the six people and two teams.

This keeps the demo compact while allowing the interface to appear highly personalised and interconnected.

# Source and Interpretation Notes

The core type structure, Three Centers, classic directions of integration/disintegration, and Nine Levels of Development are based principally on the Riso–Hudson material published by The Enneagram Institute.

The wording in this document is intentionally adapted and condensed for the demo rather than reproducing source descriptions.

The **weighted top-three Type Profile**, the six fictional personas, the development interpretations, the Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development chains, and the proposed LMS uses are **demo design constructs**. They should not be represented as validated Enneagram assessment methodology.

The Levels are particularly important to distinguish from LMS progression: completing learning content should not be presented as automatically changing a person's psychological “level.” The Levels supply a conceptual vertical-development lens; the LMS tracks learning, practice and capability development.
