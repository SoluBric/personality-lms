# Enneagram Demo App — Content Map / Blueprint

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This blueprint defines the content that should be created for the Enneagram-based demo application before the skills and course catalogue is designed.

The Enneagram is being used as **demo content** for a broader personality-results + LMS concept. The objective is therefore not to model every aspect of Enneagram theory. Instead, the content should be sufficiently rich to demonstrate:

- differentiated personality profiles;
- vertical development;
- stress and growth pathways;
- strengths and development areas;
- personalised learning recommendations;
- team interactions; and
- pathway-style learning interfaces.

The demo is expected to contain **six fictional team members**, divided into:

- **Team A:** four members; and
- **Team B:** two members.

Because the users and teams are fixed for the demo, bespoke content only needs to be created for combinations and relationships that actually appear in those six profiles.

---

# 2. Reusable Core Type Content

Create the following foundation content for **each of the nine Enneagram types**.

## 2.1 Primary Type Description

A clear overview of the type's characteristic way of orienting to the world.

## 2.2 Core Motivation / Orientation

A concise explanation of the underlying concerns, motivations or priorities associated with the type.

## 2.3 Primary Traits

A manageable set of characteristic traits suitable for profile cards and summary interfaces.

## 2.4 Primary Strengths

The type's most important constructive qualities and natural assets.

## 2.5 Primary Challenges

Common areas in which the type's normal strategy may become limiting or counterproductive.

## 2.6 Typical Blind Spots

Patterns that may be less obvious to the person themselves than to colleagues or other observers.

## 2.7 Typical Stressors

Situations or pressures that commonly make the type's normal strategy more rigid or reactive.

## 2.8 Growth Themes

Broad developmental directions associated with the type. These will later become bridges into skills and courses.

Example development-theme tags might include:

- assertiveness;
- boundaries;
- agency;
- emotional regulation;
- active listening;
- focus;
- delegation;
- adaptability;
- conflict management; and
- decision confidence.

These are **development tags at this stage**, not yet a formal skills taxonomy or course catalogue.

---

# 3. Strength → Overuse → Challenge → Development

For each type, create a concise chain showing how a genuine strength can become counterproductive when overused or applied inflexibly.

The basic structure is:

> **Strength → Possible Overuse → Resulting Challenge / Blind Spot → Development Direction**

Examples:

| Type | Strength | Possible Overuse | Possible Challenge / Blind Spot | Development Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standards | Perfectionism | "I'm simply maintaining quality." | Flexibility |
| 2 | Supportiveness | Over-helping | Own needs and boundaries become secondary | Boundaries |
| 3 | Achievement | Excessive performance/image focus | Results crowd out process or authenticity | Authenticity and listening |
| 4 | Emotional depth | Emotional absorption | Perspective and execution can diminish | Perspective and action |
| 5 | Analysis | Withdrawal / excessive preparation | "I need more information first." | Engagement and action |
| 6 | Risk awareness | Over-vigilance | "I'm just being responsible." | Calibrated trust |
| 7 | Possibility | Scattering | "I'm keeping our options open." | Focus and follow-through |
| 8 | Decisiveness | Excessive force | Others stop contributing opposing views | Receptivity |
| 9 | Harmony | Over-accommodation / avoidance | "It isn't worth making an issue of it." | Agency and assertion |

This structure should later be reusable in:

- personality profiles;
- blind-spot interfaces;
- growth pathways;
- course recommendations; and
- team-interaction content.

# 4. Weighted Type Profiles for the Six Demo Members

The demo should not rely primarily on the traditional wing model.

Each fictional team member should instead have weighted results across the nine types, with their **three strongest type patterns highlighted**.

For example:

- **Primary Type:** 9 — 86%
- **Secondary Influence:** 2 — 78%
- **Tertiary Influence:** 6 — 71%

The remaining type scores can still exist in the profile but do not require bespoke interpretive content.

## 4.1 Terminology

Because traditional Trifix/Tritype methodologies are not simply equivalent to selecting the three highest test scores, the demo should use neutral terminology such as:

- Type Profile;
- Type Blend;
- Top Type Pattern; or
- Enneagram Profile.

The final user-facing terminology can be chosen later.

## 4.2 Bespoke Profile Content

For each of the six team members, create:

- primary type;
- secondary type;
- tertiary type;
- relative weighting / scores;
- a short description of how the three patterns interact;
- combined strengths;
- possible tensions;
- converging development themes; and
- any particularly important implications for learning recommendations.

Only the **six combinations actually used by the demo characters** need to be written.

---

# 5. Nine Levels of Development

The demo should incorporate the **nine Levels of Development for each of the nine types** as its principal vertical-development framework.

This creates 81 Type × Level combinations, but each entry can remain concise.

For each Type × Level entry, create:

- **Level number**;
- **short level title**;
- **1–2 sentence description**;
- **key behavioural indicators**; and
- optionally, a **development or warning signal**.

The levels should show how the same underlying type orientation changes as it becomes more flexible and constructive or more rigid and reactive.

For example, for Type 9 the harmony orientation might broadly move through expressions such as:

> **Facilitation → Accommodation → Avoidance → Disengagement**

The precise level-by-level content will be populated later.

## 5.1 Purpose in the Demo

The Levels can support:

- vertical-development visualisation;
- recognising stress movement;
- early-warning content;
- operating-development pathways; and
- reflective learning.

The application should **not** imply that course completion automatically raises a person's psychological development level.

---

# 6. Lines of Connection

For each type, document its two connected Enneagram types.

Rather than merely recording the connection, each line should contain a compact developmental interpretation.

For each connection, include:

- connected type;
- useful qualities that may be accessed;
- possible reactive qualities;
- developmental interpretation; and
- potential skill-development themes.

For example:

## Type 9 → Type 3

**Useful qualities:**

- action;
- goal orientation;
- visibility;
- momentum.

**Development interpretation:**

Moving from accommodation or inertia toward deliberate agency and purposeful action.

## Type 9 → Type 6

**Useful qualities:**

- preparedness;
- questioning;
- responsibility;
- risk awareness.

**Possible reactive qualities:**

- worry;
- doubt;
- threat amplification.

The lines can later become navigable **Integration / Growth Pathways** containing courses, awareness material and practices.

---

# 7. Three Centers

Include concise reusable content for the three Centers.

## 7.1 Head Center

**Types 5, 6 and 7**

Broad themes include:

- thinking;
- analysis;
- anticipation;
- planning;
- ideas; and
- responses to uncertainty.

## 7.2 Heart Center

**Types 2, 3 and 4**

Broad themes include:

- relationships;
- identity;
- emotional information;
- interpersonal response;
- recognition; and
- connection.

## 7.3 Body / Instinctive Center

**Types 8, 9 and 1**

Broad themes include:

- action;
- autonomy;
- boundaries;
- instinctive response;
- standards; and
- embodied reaction.

## 7.4 Center Content for Individual Profiles

For each demo member, the application can identify:

- their primary type's Center;
- the Centers represented by their secondary and tertiary types; and
- a short interpretation of the resulting distribution.

For example, a **9 / 2 / 6** profile contains one strong pattern from each Center, whereas a **5 / 6 / 7** profile is concentrated entirely within the Head Center.

---

# 8. Individual Development Summary

Create one bespoke development summary for each of the six fictional team members.

This should synthesise the reusable type material and the person's weighted top-three profile.

Each summary should contain approximately:

## Strength to Deepen

A natural capability or orientation worth developing further.

## Pattern to Watch

An important overuse, challenge or blind spot.

## Stress Signal

A concise indicator of how the person's pattern may become less constructive under pressure.

## Growth Resource

A quality or alternative strategy worth developing, including relevant lines-of-connection material where appropriate.

## Recommended Capability Areas

A short list of development themes that will later connect to the course catalogue.

Only **six** such summaries are required.

---

# 9. Team Pairing Content

Because the demo contains fixed teams, there is no need to create every possible Enneagram type pairing.

## Team A

Four members produce **six unique pair relationships**.

## Team B

Two members produce **one unique pair relationship**.

Therefore, the demo requires only **seven bespoke pairwise relationship profiles**.

For each actual pair, create:

- natural complementarity;
- likely misunderstandings;
- communication differences;
- possible conflict points;
- what Person A can learn from Person B;
- what Person B can learn from Person A; and
- one or more joint development themes.

These pair profiles can later drive interfaces such as:

- **How We Work Together**;
- **Where We Complement Each Other**;
- **Potential Friction**;
- **What I Can Do Differently**; and
- **What We Could Practise Together**.

The pair content can take the members' top-three profiles into account rather than relying only on their primary types.

---

# 10. Team-Level Content

Create one bespoke team profile for each of the two demo teams.

Each team profile should include:

- **Team overview**;
- **dominant strengths**;
- **potential collective blind spots**;
- **Center distribution**;
- **likely communication pattern**;
- **potential sources of friction**;
- **what the team may do particularly well**;
- **what the team may struggle with**; and
- **suggested team-development themes**.

Only **two** team-level content objects are therefore required.

# 12. Content Deliberately Deferred

The following should **not** be comprehensively built yet:

- full instinctual subtype content;
- all 27 instinctual subtypes;
- every possible top-three type combination;
- every possible type-to-type team pairing;
- a complete professional skills ontology;
- a large LMS course catalogue;
- detailed course curricula;
- Hornevian and Harmonic groupings; and
- additional specialist Enneagram models.

These would increase content volume without materially improving the demo at this stage.

---

# 13. Development Themes as Temporary Tags

While the personality content is being populated, development themes should be attached as simple tags rather than immediately building a formal skills system.

For example:

## Type 9

- assertiveness;
- agency;
- prioritisation;
- productive conflict;
- facilitation.

## Type 2

- boundaries;
- assertiveness;
- self-advocacy;
- empathy;
- coaching.

Once the six fictional profiles are populated, these tags can be analysed for overlap.

The current v3 course architecture has now superseded this early planning step. The canonical catalogue is organised as:

> **one course -> one pathway -> one pathway-specific category**

Personalisation happens through separate recommendation records and learner course state, not by assigning the same canonical course to several formal pathways.

---

The sequence should therefore be:

> **Content Blueprint**
>
> ↓
>
> **Populate Core Enneagram Content**
>
> ↓
>
> **Create Six Demo Personas and Their Weighted Profiles**
>
> ↓
>
> **Create Two Team Profiles and Seven Relevant Pairings**
>
> ↓
>
> **Identify Recurring Development Themes**
>
> ↓
>
> **Use the v3 Skills and Course Taxonomy**
>
> ↓
>
> **Maintain the Canonical v3 Course Catalogue and Recommendation Seeds**

This keeps the demo deliberately bounded while ensuring that the eventual interfaces have enough underlying content to appear sophisticated, personalised and interconnected.
