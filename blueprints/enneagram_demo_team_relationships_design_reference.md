# Enneagram Demo App --- Team & Relationships Design Reference

**Document type:** Conceptual blueprint / design reference\
**Intended use:** Scaffolding and inspiration for Codex while developing
the demo application\
**Scope:** Team overview, colleague exploration, pair relationships and
shared team development

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## How to Use This Document

This is a lightweight conceptual reference for the Team area of the
demo.

It is intentionally **not a prescriptive UI specification**. Team
information could eventually be presented through pages, cards, maps,
relationship views, overlays, interactive diagrams or another approach.

The purpose of this document is mainly to describe the useful
information relationships and experiences already supported by the demo
content.

The detailed team profiles, seven pair relationships, development themes
and shared pathways are contained in the separate content files.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Overall Concept

The Team area broadly answers:

> **How do we tend to operate together?**

It extends the individual Profile and Development experiences into a
social context.

The learner may be able to explore:

-   who is in their team;
-   selected information about colleagues' profiles;
-   what different people naturally contribute;
-   how two colleagues may complement one another;
-   where misunderstandings or friction may arise;
-   how the same situation can look different from another person's
    perspective;
-   what the team appears strong in collectively;
-   where shared development may help;
-   which courses or pathways connect to team needs.

The aim is not to create a compatibility score.

It is to provide **useful perspectives for working together more
deliberately**.

# 3. Team Overview

## Purpose

Give the learner a quick sense of the team as a whole before focusing on
individual relationships.

The overview may communicate:

-   who is on the team;
-   each member's strongest patterns;
-   the range of perspectives represented;
-   collective strengths;
-   recurring team tensions;
-   shared development priorities;
-   current team pathway.

## Possible presentation ideas

Potential approaches include:

-   member cards;
-   a team constellation or network;
-   a shared nine-type visualisation;
-   profile markers arranged around a team space;
-   collective pattern summaries;
-   a combination of conventional cards and interactive relationship
    links.

The existing demo's idea of showing team members alongside shared
strengths / underrepresented areas is useful inspiration and can be
retained, adapted or replaced.

## Considerations

The team overview should avoid implying that the team's effectiveness
can be reduced to the arithmetic sum of personality scores.

The value lies in **patterns of contribution and interaction**, not a
single team score.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Collective Team Strengths

## Purpose

Show what the team's mix of working styles may enable.

For Team Atlas, the content already suggests a useful combination of:

-   relational awareness;
-   decisive action;
-   creative exploration;
-   analytical depth;
-   quality orientation;
-   risk awareness;
-   delivery energy.

For Team Beacon, the combination includes:

-   opportunity recognition;
-   persuasion and momentum;
-   synthesis;
-   feasibility;
-   operational judgement.

## Possible presentation ideas

Collective strengths could be represented through:

-   themes;
-   capability clusters;
-   short explanatory observations;
-   links to the team members who contribute strongly to each area.

The emphasis can remain practical:

> **What does this mix help us do well?**

rather than simply:

> **Which Enneagram numbers do we have?**

# 5. Team Development Themes

## Purpose

Identify recurring patterns that may benefit from shared development.

For Team Atlas, examples already established include:

-   making disagreement visible earlier;
-   improving decision closure;
-   protecting both quality and momentum.

For Team Beacon:

-   earlier challenge;
-   disciplined opportunity selection;
-   credible external commitments.

These themes can provide a bridge between personality information and
shared learning.

## Possible presentation ideas

Themes might appear as:

-   team observations;
-   development priorities;
-   challenges to explore;
-   pathway entry points;
-   linked relationship patterns.

The exact number shown at once can remain small.

# 6. Colleague Exploration

## Purpose

Allow a learner to understand a colleague's working style without
exposing that person's entire private profile.

A colleague view broadly answers:

> **What might be useful for me to understand about how this person
> tends to operate?**

## Information that may be useful

Depending on the eventual privacy model, a colleague view might include:

-   name and role;
-   primary / top-three patterns;
-   characteristic working strengths;
-   preferred contribution style;
-   likely communication tendencies;
-   possible friction points;
-   "working well with me" guidance;
-   shared development opportunities.

## Possible presentation ideas

A colleague could be opened from:

-   the team overview;
-   a relationship map;
-   a member card;
-   a team constellation;
-   another contextual interaction.

The exact amount of Enneagram detail shown to colleagues can remain
flexible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Privacy and Boundaries

The Team experience should distinguish between:

**information useful for collaboration**

and

**private personal-development information**.

Potentially useful shared information:

-   working strengths;
-   broad profile patterns;
-   collaboration preferences;
-   useful communication guidance;
-   shared development themes.

Information that may be better kept private by default:

-   detailed stress reflections;
-   private self-assessment responses;
-   sensitive blind-spot notes;
-   inferred development-level placement;
-   personal reflections intended only for the learner.

The demo does not need a sophisticated permissions system, but the
conceptual distinction is useful.

The Team area should feel like a tool for collaboration rather than
psychological surveillance.

# 8. Pair Relationships

## Purpose

Explore what may happen when two different operating styles meet.

The existing content contains seven actual pair relationships:

### Team Atlas

-   Emily ↔ James
-   Emily ↔ Sophie
-   Emily ↔ Thomas
-   James ↔ Sophie
-   James ↔ Thomas
-   Sophie ↔ Thomas

### Team Beacon

-   Sarah ↔ Oliver

Each relationship already has enough content for a focused demo
interaction.

## Information that may be useful

A pair relationship may include:

-   natural complementarity;
-   likely misunderstanding;
-   possible friction;
-   what Person A may learn from Person B;
-   what Person B may learn from Person A;
-   what each person should keep in mind;
-   a joint development theme;
-   shared courses.

The interface does not need to expose all of this simultaneously.

# 9. Complementarity

## Purpose

Show that difference can be useful.

For example, Emily and James combine:

**inclusion + decisive action**

Thomas and James combine:

**analytical rigour + action**

Sarah and Oliver combine:

**operational judgement + opportunity**

This is more useful than labelling relationships as simply "compatible"
or "incompatible."

## Possible presentation ideas

A relationship could highlight:

> **What you bring**

> **What they bring**

> **What becomes possible together**

This may provide a positive entry point before discussing friction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Misunderstanding and Friction

## Purpose

Help learners recognise predictable differences before interpreting them
as bad intent.

For Emily and James, the content identifies a central misunderstanding:

> James may think the decision is settled before Emily has actually
> expressed her disagreement.

For Sarah and Oliver:

> Oliver may believe Sarah is applying the brakes too late; Sarah may
> believe Oliver accelerated before the road was checked.

These kinds of observations are particularly useful because they
describe **interaction patterns**, not personality defects.

## Possible presentation ideas

The interface could distinguish between:

-   what happens;
-   how I may interpret it;
-   how the other person may interpret it;
-   what could interrupt the pattern.

A small number of strong observations may be more effective than
exhaustive relationship analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Perspective Switching

One of the most promising interaction ideas for the Team area is
allowing the learner to view a relationship from the colleague's
perspective.

For example:

### Emily viewing James

> His directness may be intended as efficiency and clarity rather than
> hostility.

Then:

### See it from James's perspective

> Emily's initial agreement may not mean that she has fully committed to
> the decision. She may need explicit space to state disagreement.

The exact interface could be:

-   a perspective toggle;
-   flip interaction;
-   side-by-side comparison;
-   conversational view;
-   another mechanism entirely.

The important concept is:

> **The same interaction can look different depending on where you
> stand.**

This could become one of the strongest moments in the demo.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Practical "Try This" Guidance

## Purpose

Move relationship insight toward behaviour.

A relationship view may include one or two practical experiments.

Examples:

### For James working with Emily

> Ask for opposing views before announcing the preferred decision.

### For Emily working with James

> State your disagreement while the decision is still open rather than
> waiting until consensus appears established.

### For Oliver working with Sarah

> Ask what delivery constraint could make the opportunity unrealistic
> before moving from interest to commitment.

These do not need to become formal courses.

They can simply demonstrate that personality insight can generate
practical behavioural guidance.

Relationship insight can connect to learning.

-   TM-GEN-01 Listening Before Leading;

**Opportunity with disciplined commitment**

-   TM-GEN-04 Role Clarity and Ownership;
-   TM-GEN-07 High-Trust Team Commitments.

# 15. Individual + Relationship + Team Intersections

A particularly important concept is that the same capability or theme may matter at several levels, while each course remains assigned to one canonical pathway.

For example:

### Productive Disagreement

**Emily's personal development**\
because she may delay visible disagreement;

**Emily ↔ James relationship development**\
because their styles can create an avoidance / domination cycle;

**Team Atlas development**\
because the team benefits from surfacing disagreement before decision
closure.

This is a strong example of the platform connecting:

**individual insight + relationship dynamics + team learning**

without returning to the old multi-pathway course model.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Viewer-Specific Team Guidance

The same team problem may produce different guidance for each member.

Example Team Atlas issue:

> **We are taking too long to make project decisions.**

Emily may see guidance about stating her own position rather than only
facilitating others.

James may see guidance about creating space for dissent before closure.

Sophie may see guidance about moving from exploration into an
experiment.

Thomas may see guidance about identifying which information would
materially change the decision.

This is a useful demonstration of personalised development within a
shared team context.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. What the Team Area Does Not Need

For the current demo, there is little need for:

-   exhaustive compatibility scoring;
-   every possible type pairing;
-   a complete organisational network;
-   sophisticated permissions;
-   manager analytics;
-   performance-management scoring;
-   team personality "grades";
-   predictions about interpersonal success;
-   extensive collaboration tooling.

The six personas and seven actual relationships are enough to
demonstrate the idea.

# 19. What Matters Most for the Demo

The highest-value Team concepts are likely to be:

1.  seeing who is in the team and their broad profile differences;
2.  understanding what the team mix may contribute collectively;
3.  seeing a small number of meaningful shared development themes;
4.  opening a colleague relationship;
5.  seeing complementarity rather than a compatibility score;
6.  identifying a likely misunderstanding or friction pattern;
7.  switching perspective between the two people;
8.  receiving one or two practical behavioural suggestions;
9.  seeing shared learning recommendations;
10. connecting individual, relationship and team development.

Everything else can be simplified.

# 20. Example Demo Story

Emily opens **Team Atlas**.

She sees:

-   herself;
-   James;
-   Sophie;
-   Thomas;
-   a summary of the team's diverse working strengths;
-   a team development theme around making disagreement visible earlier.

She opens **James**.

The relationship view explains:

> Emily brings inclusion and relational awareness. James brings
> decisiveness and momentum.

It then surfaces the likely pattern:

> James may think a decision is settled before Emily has actually
> expressed her disagreement.

Emily switches perspective:

> James may experience Emily's apparent agreement as confirmation that
> the issue has been resolved.

A practical suggestion appears:

> State disagreement while the decision is still open.

The relationship links to:

**TM-GEN-02 Productive Disagreement**

The learner can then see that the same capability also matters for:

**Emily's personal development**

and:

**Team Atlas --- From Diverse Perspectives to Confident Decisions**

This journey demonstrates how individual insight, relationship context
and Team-pathway learning can connect while each course keeps its
canonical v3 pathway assignment.

# 21. Core Experience

The Team area is successful if the learner broadly leaves with these
impressions:

### "Different working styles can be useful."

Difference is presented as complementarity as well as friction.

### "I can understand how the other person may see this."

Perspective switching reduces simplistic interpretation.

### "There are practical things I can do differently."

Insight leads toward behaviour.

### "Our team has development patterns, not just individual profiles."

The group becomes a meaningful development context.

### "Capabilities connect across levels."

Personal, relationship and team needs can intersect, while courses keep
their canonical v3 pathway assignment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. Scope Reminder

This blueprint is intentionally provisional and incomplete.

The Team area has significant room for creative interpretation.

The detailed content already provides enough material for the demo; the
interface does not need to expose all of it.

The goal is to use the available personality and development information
to create a **useful, professional and interesting way of exploring
collaboration**, while leaving Codex free to discover the clearest
visual and interaction model.
