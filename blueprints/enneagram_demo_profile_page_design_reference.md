# Enneagram Demo App — Profile Page Design Reference

**Document type:** Conceptual blueprint / design reference  
**Intended use:** Scaffolding and inspiration for Codex while developing the demo application  
**Scope:** Individual Profile area

---

## How to Use This Document

This document is a conceptual reference for the Profile area of the demo application. It describes the information available, the ideas the interface may need to communicate, and some possible presentation or interaction patterns.

It is intentionally **not a prescriptive UI specification**.

Suggested layouts, components and interactions are examples rather than requirements. They may be combined, simplified, reinterpreted or omitted where a clearer solution emerges during development.

The priority is to preserve the useful **conceptual relationships, information hierarchy and user experience goals**, rather than reproduce any particular proposed layout.

The existing demo screenshots are useful inspiration and demonstrate several concepts that already work, but they are not a required visual template.

This blueprint should be read alongside the separate research and content files. Those files contain the underlying Enneagram material and demo content; this document mainly provides a possible way of thinking about how that material might come together in the Profile experience.

---

# 1. Overall Profile Concept

## Purpose

The Profile area broadly answers:

> **How do I tend to operate?**

It can act as the interpretation layer between the learner's assessment results and the development / learning areas of the platform.

The learner should be able to understand their strongest patterns without feeling confined to a single type.

A useful guiding idea is:

> **Your strongest patterns are a starting point, not a boundary.**

The Enneagram result can therefore be presented as a profile across the complete nine-type system rather than simply as a label.

## Information that may be useful

The Profile area has access to:

- learner name, role and team;
- scores across all nine types;
- primary, secondary and tertiary patterns;
- combined top-three / trifix interpretation;
- type descriptions and traits;
- strengths and challenges;
- overuse patterns and blind spots;
- the nine vertical development levels;
- Enneagram connection lines;
- related capacities and skills;
- development recommendations.

## General considerations

The Profile should distinguish between:

**personality-pattern prominence**  
and  
**trainable professional capability**.

An assessment score is not a skill score.

A lower Type 8 result, for example, does not mean the learner possesses “43% assertiveness.” Instead, Type 8 may represent capacities such as directness, boundary protection and willingness to confront that could be useful for the learner to develop.

# 2. Profile Overview

## Purpose

Give the learner an immediate sense of their overall pattern.

The strongest three patterns are likely to deserve particular prominence, while the complete nine-type system remains visible or easy to access.

The overview should help communicate:

- what patterns appear most strongly;
- which pattern is primary;
- how the top three combine;
- that the remaining types are still relevant and explorable.

## Information that may be useful

- learner identity and role;
- primary / secondary / tertiary types;
- assessment scores;
- combined-profile title or shorthand;
- short combined-profile interpretation;
- complete nine-type distribution.

## Possible presentation ideas

The circular nine-type visualisation in the existing demo is one useful reference. It keeps the whole system visible while allowing the strongest patterns to stand out.

Other possibilities could include:

- radial or constellation-style arrangements;
- a ranked visual combined with a type map;
- a more abstract pattern visualisation;
- a combination of graphic and textual summary.

The top three could receive stronger visual prominence without making the other six feel irrelevant.

A selected type could potentially become an exploration focus while the learner's actual profile remains unchanged.

## Connections

This area naturally connects to:

- Pattern Distribution;
- the top-three combination;
- deeper exploration of any type;
- the learner's development recommendations.

## Considerations

The learner should not leave the overview thinking:

> “I am only this type.”

The stronger message is:

> “These are the patterns I currently draw on most readily.”

---

# 3. Pattern Distribution & Type Exploration

## Purpose

Show the learner's complete assessment result and make all nine patterns meaningful.

This is also an opportunity to reinforce the idea that lower-ranked patterns are not “bad” results.

## Information that may be useful

For each type:

- type number;
- type name;
- assessment score;
- relative rank;
- core orientation;
- characteristic strengths;
- characteristic challenges;
- useful capacities;
- related skills.

## Possible presentation ideas

The existing ranked-bar view is a useful reference and may be retained, adapted or replaced.

The learner might be able to inspect any row or type and see additional information.

A contextual type inspector could contain material such as:

- what this pattern tends to notice;
- strengths associated with it;
- what can happen when it is overused;
- professional capacities associated with it;
- how prominent it is in this learner's assessment.

For a type outside the learner's top three, the interface could frame it as an additional perspective or source of useful capacities.

Example:

> **Type 8 is less prominent in your current profile, but its emphasis on directness and boundary protection may provide useful resources in situations requiring firm action.**

## Connections

Type exploration can connect naturally to:

- Development Levels;
- connection lines;
- associated skills;
- development pathways.

## Considerations

Terminology such as **Pattern Distribution** or **Assessment Pattern** is preferable to **Type Strengths** if the latter could imply that the score measures competence.

# 4. How the Strongest Patterns Combine

## Purpose

Explain why the learner's top-three profile is more interesting than three independent type descriptions.

For the demo, this is where the trifix-style approach can provide useful variation between team members without requiring exhaustive coverage of every theoretical combination.

## Information that may be useful

- primary pattern;
- secondary pattern;
- tertiary pattern;
- role each plays in the combined profile;
- pair interactions;
- reinforcing qualities;
- possible tensions or overuse;
- bespoke combined interpretation.

## Possible presentation ideas

The three patterns could be explored individually and in combination.

For example, a 9 / 2 / 6 profile might consider:

**9 + 2**  
Interpersonal awareness, support and accommodation.

**9 + 6**  
Stability, loyalty, preparation and sensitivity to uncertainty.

**2 + 6**  
Responsiveness to people's needs and concerns.

The interface might then return to a single integrated description showing how these influences appear together.

This could be visual, textual, interactive or a combination.

## Connections

This section naturally feeds into:

- characteristic working patterns;
- strengths;
- overuse tendencies;
- blind spots;
- personalised development recommendations.

## Considerations

The combined profile is demo content rather than a claim that every possible three-type combination has a formally established interpretation.

The goal is a coherent and useful demonstration of how multiple assessment dimensions could later be combined in the real platform.

# 5. How You Tend to Operate

## Purpose

Translate personality theory into practical, recognisable behaviour.

This is likely to be one of the areas where the learner most strongly experiences:

> “Yes, I recognise this.”

## Information that may be useful

Potential content includes:

- what tends to get the learner's attention;
- typical motivations;
- working style;
- interpersonal tendencies;
- decision tendencies;
- characteristic strengths;
- possible challenges;
- strength overuse;
- blind spots;
- early stress patterns.

## Possible presentation ideas

This material could appear through:

- concise cards;
- expandable observations;
- grouped themes;
- short behavioural statements;
- linked chains showing how one pattern leads to another.

One useful conceptual relationship is:

**Strength → Possible Overuse → Consequence / Blind Spot → Development Opportunity**

For example:

**Harmony building**  
→ **Over-accommodation**  
→ **Delayed disagreement**  
→ **Assertive Communication**

This relationship could eventually continue into learning:

**Assertive Communication**  
→ **F8-01 Assertiveness and Clean Boundaries**

The exact presentation is open.

## Connections

This is one of the strongest bridges between Profile and Pathways.

An insight may connect to:

- a skill;
- a development theme;
- a course;
- a longer pathway.

## Considerations

Blind spots should generally be framed as possibilities rather than certainties.

Useful language includes:

- may;
- can;
- watch for;
- consider whether;
- under some circumstances.

Development is better framed as increasing flexibility than fixing defects.

# 6. Development Levels

## Purpose

Show that a type is not a fixed behavioural state.

The nine vertical levels can demonstrate how the same underlying pattern may appear in more constructive, flexible forms or in increasingly reactive and constrained forms.

This is important to the broader philosophy of the demo.

## Information that may be useful

For each of the nine types:

- nine development levels;
- characteristic mindset at each level;
- behavioural signs;
- likely risks;
- possible developmental direction.

## Possible presentation ideas

A vertical ladder or continuum is an obvious possibility, but not a requirement.

The interface could distinguish broadly between:

- more flexible / constructive expression;
- more imbalanced or reactive expression;
- highly constrained expression.

The learner's primary type could be the initial focus, while allowing another type to be explored.

Individual levels might reveal more detail when selected.

## Connections

Development Levels may connect to:

- stress awareness;
- self-reflection;
- relevant skills;
- learning recommendations;
- other type exploration.

## Considerations

The demo should avoid presenting an unsupported precise claim such as:

> “You are currently Level 5.”

The levels are primarily an **exploration and development framework** unless a separate assessment mechanism genuinely supports placement.

# 7. Connections & Expanding Your Range

## Purpose

Use Enneagram connection lines and the wider nine-type model to reinforce behavioural flexibility.

The important idea is not that the learner must “become another type.”

Rather:

> **Other patterns can represent useful capacities that may broaden the learner's available responses.**

## Information that may be useful

For the learner's primary type:

- connected types;
- possible resources from those types;
- stress-related tendencies;
- alternative ways of responding.

The broader type library also contains capacities that may complement the learner's dominant patterns even where there is no formal connection line.

## Possible presentation ideas

The traditional Enneagram connection diagram is one reference.

Connection information could also be presented as:

- growth resources;
- alternative response modes;
- “qualities to draw on”;
- expandable relationship cards.

For a Type 9 learner, Type 3 might highlight resources such as:

- visible agency;
- goal-directed action;
- explicit priorities;
- ownership.

A Type 8 exploration might highlight:

- directness;
- boundary protection;
- willingness to confront.

The interface could explain why these qualities may be useful in relation to the learner's own profile.

## Connections

This area can lead directly into:

- development skills;
- growth pathways;
- stress-management learning;
- the broader Explore-the-Nine experience.

## Considerations

Connection lines do not need to be reduced to a simplistic “good direction / bad direction.”

The demo can use them more broadly as developmental and contextual resources.

# 8. Exploring All Nine Types

## Purpose

Keep the entire Enneagram model accessible throughout the experience.

This supports the philosophy that all nine patterns contain potentially useful capacities and prevents the assessment result from becoming a restrictive identity label.

## Information that may be useful

For every type:

- core orientation;
- short description;
- characteristic strengths;
- characteristic challenges;
- overuse tendencies;
- useful capacities;
- development levels;
- connection information;
- related skills.

## Possible presentation ideas

Exploration could exist as:

- a dedicated section;
- a persistent type selector;
- an inspector or drawer;
- links from the main visualisation;
- a reusable type browser available across several Profile sections.

It does not necessarily need to become one large separate component.

A particularly useful optional layer is:

### How this pattern may complement your profile

This could contextualise another type for the current learner.

Example:

> Type 8 capacities such as directness and boundary protection may complement a profile that more naturally prioritises harmony and accommodation.

## Connections

All-nine exploration can connect to nearly every other Profile concept:

- distribution;
- levels;
- connections;
- skills;
- development.

## Considerations

Exploring another type should not accidentally imply that the learner's stored assessment result has changed.

There is a distinction between:

**My profile**  
and  
**The pattern I am currently exploring.**

# 9. From Profile to Development

## Purpose

Turn insight into possible action without turning the Profile page itself into the LMS.

This section broadly answers:

> **What might I do with what I have learned about myself?**

## Information that may be useful

Potential development directions include the four v3 course pathways:

### Strengths

Develop an existing natural capability further.

### Stress & Growth

Recognise stress patterns and expand more constructive responses.

### Fortification

Develop skills that compensate for common challenges or blind spots.

### Team

Apply profile insight to collaboration with other people.

## Possible presentation ideas

The Profile might surface a small number of development entry points rather than the entire learning plan.

For example:

**Strengthen an existing capability**  
→ facilitation / perspective integration

**Expand your range**  
→ assertiveness / boundaries

**Build resilience under pressure**  
→ stress awareness / decisions under uncertainty

**Develop with your team**  
→ Team Atlas collaboration

These could lead into the separate Pathways area.

## Connections

This is the main transition from:

**Profile → Skills → Courses → Pathways**

It may also link to:

**Profile → Team**

## Considerations

The Profile should explain **why** a development direction may be relevant.

The Pathways area can handle the more detailed learning route.

# 10. Possible Page-Level Navigation

The Profile may become relatively long.

Some form of local navigation could therefore be useful, although its exact form is open.

Potential conceptual destinations include:

- Overview
- Distribution
- Combination
- Patterns
- Levels
- Connections
- Development

This could be implemented through navigation, scrolling, contextual controls or another approach.

It is not necessary for each conceptual area to become a visually separate section if a more coherent interface emerges.

# 11. Useful Terminology

The following language broadly supports the intended philosophy.

## Potentially useful

- Primary pattern
- Strong supporting pattern
- Pattern distribution
- Assessment score
- Characteristic capacity
- Development opportunity
- Blind spot
- Growth resource
- Expand your range
- Explore another pattern
- More characteristic / less characteristic
- More readily available response

## Language to use cautiously

- Weak type
- Bad type
- Skill score derived from personality score
- Fixed compatibility
- “You cannot…”
- deterministic behavioural predictions
- precise development-level placement without evidence

# 12. What Matters Most for the Demo

The blueprint contains more ideas than necessarily need to appear in the first implementation.

The highest-value concepts are likely to be:

1. a compelling overall Profile view;
2. all nine patterns remaining visible or easily accessible;
3. clear prominence for the top three;
4. meaningful combined top-three interpretation;
5. practical strengths, challenges, overuse and blind spots;
6. the nine vertical development levels;
7. connection-line / growth-resource exploration;
8. easy exploration of other types;
9. a clear bridge from personality insight into skills and pathways.

Everything else can be simplified, combined or deferred if doing so produces a stronger demo.

---

# 13. Core Experience

The Profile area is successful if the learner broadly leaves with four impressions:

### “This feels recognisably about me.”

The personalised combination and behavioural interpretation create relevance.

### “I am more than one type.”

The complete nine-pattern system remains accessible.

### “These patterns are not fixed limitations.”

Levels, connections and other-type capacities show movement and flexibility.

### “I can do something with this.”

Profile insights can lead naturally toward trainable skills and learning pathways.

---

# 14. Relationship to the Wider Handover Material

This blueprint is intended to sit in a **Blueprints / Design Reference** collection.

The detailed Enneagram research, persona content, course catalogue, team content and other source material can sit separately in a **Research / Content** collection.

A later project-level handover can explain to Codex:

- what the different collections contain;
- which documents are conceptual research versus demo content;
- which documents are blueprints;
- what is already established;
- what remains deliberately open;
- how much freedom Codex has to reinterpret the material.

The intended relationship is:

**Research and content provide the material.**  
**Blueprints provide optional structure and product thinking.**  
**The final handover provides orientation.**  
**Codex retains implementation and design freedom.**

The purpose of the documentation is therefore to **enhance the solution space with context**, not narrow it prematurely.
