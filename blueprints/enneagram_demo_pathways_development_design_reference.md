# Enneagram Demo App --- Pathways & Development Design Reference

**Document type:** Conceptual blueprint / design reference\
**Intended use:** Scaffolding and inspiration for Codex while developing
the demo application\
**Scope:** Development / Pathways area

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## How to Use This Document

This document describes some of the ideas, information relationships and
possible experiences that could inform the Development / Pathways area
of the demo.

It is intentionally **not a prescriptive UI specification**. The
eventual interface does not need to reproduce any particular map, skill
tree, card layout, progression mechanic or metaphor described here.

The underlying content and relationships matter more than any proposed
presentation.

The research and content files provide the actual demo material. This
blueprint is intended to help show how that material might connect into
a coherent development experience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Overall Concept

The Development / Pathways area broadly answers:

> **What could I develop, why might it matter to me, and how could the
> different learning opportunities fit together?**

The aim is to move beyond a conventional LMS course catalogue.

Rather than simply presenting a list of courses, the demo can show
relationships between:

**Profile insights → Development areas → Skills → Courses → Pathways**

Recommendations may also arise from different contexts:

-   the learner's personality profile;
-   an existing strength worth developing further;
-   a challenge or blind spot;
-   stress and growth patterns;
-   a chosen development goal;
-   a relationship with a colleague;
-   a wider team-development need.

The result could feel more like a **development landscape** than a
catalogue.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Four Development Territories

The content already supports four broad development territories. These
are useful organising concepts, although they do not necessarily need to
become four literal interface columns or sections.

## Strengthen Strengths

Build further on capabilities that already come relatively naturally.

Examples might include:

-   turning good listening into advanced facilitation;
-   developing natural relationship awareness into collaborative
    influence;
-   converting analytical strength into strategic judgement.

The message is:

> **Development is not only about correcting weaknesses.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Optimise Stress Management & Growth

Explore how a learner operates under pressure and how they can increase
flexibility.

This may draw on:

-   stress awareness;
-   the nine development levels;
-   connection-line resources;
-   emotional regulation;
-   decision-making under uncertainty;
-   alternative behavioural responses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Fortify Growth Areas

Develop capabilities that may compensate for recurring challenges,
overuse patterns or blind spots.

Examples might include:

-   boundaries;
-   assertive communication;
-   constructive conflict;
-   prioritisation;
-   moving from analysis to action.

The framing should remain developmental rather than deficit-based.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Team Development

Develop capabilities that become particularly relevant through
collaboration with others.

These may arise from:

-   a specific colleague relationship;
-   recurring team friction;
-   complementary working styles;
-   team decision patterns;
-   shared development goals.

This territory naturally connects the individual Development area with
the Team area.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. The Main Building Blocks

Several distinct concepts are available to the Pathways experience.

Keeping them conceptually separate creates flexibility even if the
interface later blends them together.

## Development Theme / Territory

A broad reason or context for development.

Example:

**Fortify Growth Areas**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Skill

A trainable capability.

Examples:

-   Assertive Communication
-   Active Listening
-   Facilitation
-   Risk Judgement
-   Prioritisation
-   Collaborative Influence

A skill can be developed through more than one course or experience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Course

A learning intervention.

Example:

**Finding Your Voice**

A course may develop several skills and may appear in several pathways.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Pathway

An intentional sequence or network of development experiences.

Example:

**From Accommodation to Constructive Assertion**

A pathway helps explain how separate learning opportunities fit
together.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Recommendation

The personalised explanation for why a particular skill, course or
pathway may be relevant.

This is especially important because the **same course can be
recommended to different people for different reasons**.

The intelligence of the demo therefore lies partly in:

> not only **what** is recommended, but **why**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Personal Development Landscape

## Purpose

Give the learner an understandable view of their development
opportunities without implying that every recommendation has equal
importance.

The landscape may help them see:

-   what already comes naturally;
-   where greater range could be useful;
-   which skills connect to which insights;
-   how courses relate to skills;
-   where different pathways overlap;
-   what they might explore next.

## Possible presentation ideas

There is deliberately no required visual model.

Possibilities include:

-   a development map;
-   a professional skill tree;
-   a constellation or network;
-   connected pathways;
-   territories or regions;
-   a journey;
-   a subtle forging / capability-building metaphor;
-   a hybrid of map and conventional interface elements.

The eventual solution can remain professional while borrowing the
satisfying structural qualities of RPG progression systems.

The metaphor should support understanding rather than dominate it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Personalised Recommendation Logic

## Purpose

Explain why the learner sees particular development opportunities.

A recommendation might be generated from several kinds of insight.

### Profile-based

> Your stronger Type 9 pattern suggests that preserving harmony may
> sometimes delay expression of your own position.

This could connect toward:

**Assertive Communication → Finding Your Voice**

### Strength-based

> Perspective integration already appears to be a natural capability.
> Developing facilitation could extend this into a more advanced team
> skill.

### Blind-spot based

> You may not always notice when agreement has been achieved by reducing
> visible disagreement rather than resolving it.

This might connect toward:

**Constructive Conflict**

### Stress / growth based

A connection-line or development-level insight may suggest a particular
capability.

### Team-based

> Your working relationship with James may benefit from making
> disagreement visible earlier.

This could connect toward a shared course.

### Goal-based

If the learner chooses a goal such as:

> **Become more confident in difficult conversations**

the same profile may produce a different prioritisation of courses.

## Considerations

The demo does not need a sophisticated recommendation engine.

The important concept is simply that recommendations have **reasons and
provenance**, rather than appearing as unexplained course suggestions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Pathways

## Purpose

Show how development can unfold over time.

A pathway may be linear, branching or loosely connected depending on the
content.

It does not necessarily imply that every course must be completed in a
rigid order.

## Example

A possible Emily pathway:

### From Accommodation to Constructive Assertion

**Healthy Boundaries**\
→ **Finding Your Voice**\
→ **Constructive Conflict**\
→ **Difficult Conversations**

Another pathway could build on an existing strength:

**Active Listening**\
→ **Collaborative Influence**\
→ **Facilitating Productive Disagreement**

These routes demonstrate two different forms of development:

-   strengthening something already present;
-   building capacity in a less natural area.

## Possible pathway information

A pathway might expose:

-   pathway name;
-   development purpose;
-   contributing profile insight;
-   skills involved;
-   courses;
-   suggested sequence;
-   optional branches;
-   progress;
-   related team applications.

The exact amount shown at once can remain flexible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Overlap and Reuse

One of the strongest concepts in the demo is that learning objects are
reusable.

A course does not belong exclusively to one type or pathway.

For example:

**Constructive Conflict**

might appear because of:

-   Emily's personal development;
-   the Emily ↔ James relationship;
-   Team Atlas's shared development needs.

Similarly, **Collaborative Influence** can be relevant to several
learners for different reasons.

This is useful to make visible somewhere in the experience because it
demonstrates how the eventual platform could connect assessment,
learning and team data without requiring bespoke courses for every
outcome.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Learner Goals and Agency

## Purpose

Recommendations should not be the only way development is chosen.

The learner may also be able to express what they want to work on.

Possible goals might include:

-   become more confident in difficult conversations;
-   improve leadership;
-   make decisions more confidently;
-   improve prioritisation;
-   work more effectively with the team;
-   strengthen an existing capability.

This creates a useful conceptual combination:

**Assessment insight + learner goal + team context**

The resulting pathway can therefore feel guided rather than imposed.

## Considerations

This does not need to become a complex goal-management system in the
demo.

Even a lightweight indication that the learner can influence their route
would demonstrate the principle.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Progress and Development States

## Purpose

Give the development landscape a sense of movement without pretending
that course completion precisely measures personality change.

Several kinds of progress can coexist:

### Course progress

Example:

**65% complete**

### Course completion

Example:

**Completed**

### Pathway progress

Example:

**3 of 5 learning experiences completed**

### Skill-development state

Possible lightweight states might include:

**Identified → Exploring → Developing → Practised → Strengthened**

These are conceptual possibilities rather than required labels.

## Considerations

Avoid pseudo-precision such as:

> "Your Assertiveness increased from 42% to 68%."

Completing a course does not establish that kind of psychological or
behavioural measurement.

A richer but safer representation might show evidence of engagement:

-   learning completed;
-   practice undertaken;
-   reflection completed;
-   related courses completed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. A Touch of Progression / RPG Logic

The demo may benefit from some of the structural satisfaction found in
games without becoming visually game-like.

Potentially useful ideas include:

-   unlocking or illuminating connected nodes;
-   seeing pathways grow as learning progresses;
-   revealing adjacent capabilities;
-   completing clusters;
-   building or "forging" capabilities over time;
-   choosing between development branches;
-   seeing individual and team paths intersect.

These are **inspiration rather than requirements**.

The professional tone of the platform remains important.

The underlying feeling could be:

> **I am building a broader capability set.**

rather than:

> **I am collecting points.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Relationship to the Profile Area

The Profile area explains:

> **How do I tend to operate?**

The Pathways area takes selected insights and asks:

> **What might I develop from here?**

Useful transitions may therefore include:

**Profile strength**\
→ advanced skill

**Profile overuse**\
→ balancing skill

**Blind spot**\
→ awareness / practice

**Development level insight**\
→ stress or growth learning

**Connection-line resource**\
→ alternative capability

**Other-type exploration**\
→ skill that expands behavioural range

The learner should be able to understand where a recommendation came
from if they want to inspect it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Relationship to Courses

Pathways organise learning; courses provide learning experiences.

A course may show:

-   what it develops;
-   why it is recommended;
-   where it sits in a pathway;
-   what may come before or after it;
-   where else it is relevant.

The Pathways area need not reproduce detailed course information.

It can provide enough context for the learner to decide whether to
explore the course.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Relationship to Team Development

Individual and team development can intersect.

For example:

**Emily personal pathway**\
contains **Constructive Conflict**

while:

**Emily ↔ James relationship development**\
also recommends **Constructive Conflict**

and:

**Team Atlas pathway**\
contains **Constructive Conflict**

This creates an interesting intersection:

``` text
PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT
                      CONSTRUCTIVE CONFLICT
         /
        /
TEAM DEVELOPMENT
```

The eventual interface may choose to make these intersections visible.

This is potentially one of the strongest demonstrations of the
platform's underlying model.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Possible Information Available for a Skill

If a skill becomes an explorable object, useful information may include:

-   skill name;
-   short description;
-   development territory;
-   why it is relevant to this learner;
-   related profile insights;
-   contributing courses;
-   related pathways;
-   team relevance;
-   current development state.

A dedicated Skills page is not required. Skills may work better as nodes
or contextual objects inside other experiences.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Possible Information Available for a Pathway

A pathway may contain:

-   title;
-   short purpose;
-   why it is relevant;
-   associated development territory;
-   related skills;
-   course sequence or network;
-   optional branches;
-   current progress;
-   team connections;
-   possible next steps.

Again, the interface can decide how much of this is useful to expose.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. What Matters Most for the Demo

The first implementation does not need to demonstrate every possible
relationship.

The highest-value ideas are likely to be:

1.  development feels connected rather than like a flat course
    catalogue;
2.  recommendations have understandable reasons;
3.  strengths can be developed as well as growth areas;
4.  profile insights can lead to trainable skills;
5.  skills can lead to courses;
6.  courses can belong to pathways;
7.  the same course can serve different people and contexts;
8.  personal and team development can intersect;
9.  the learner has some agency in choosing where to develop;
10. progress feels meaningful without pretending to measure personality
    change precisely.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Example Demo Story

Emily provides a useful demonstration of the system.

Her Profile identifies:

**Harmony building** as a strength.

That can lead in one direction toward:

**Perspective Integration → Facilitation**

At the same time, the possible overuse of accommodation can lead toward:

**Assertive Communication → Finding Your Voice → Constructive Conflict**

Her relationship with James independently creates another reason for:

**Constructive Conflict**

Team Atlas also includes that course within:

### From Diverse Perspectives to Confident Decisions

The learner can therefore discover that one learning experience has
several layers of relevance:

> **For me**\
> **For this working relationship**\
> **For my team**

This is a strong example of the wider platform concept and does not
require a large amount of course content to demonstrate it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Core Experience

The Development / Pathways area is successful if the learner broadly
leaves with these impressions:

### "I understand why these development opportunities are here."

Recommendations are connected to recognisable insights.

### "There are different directions I could take."

The system guides without locking the learner into one route.

### "My strengths are part of development too."

Learning is not framed purely as remediation.

### "The courses fit together."

Learning experiences form meaningful routes rather than an arbitrary
catalogue.

### "My development connects to how I work with other people."

Personal, relationship and team development can intersect.

### "I am building range rather than changing who I am."

The end goal is greater behavioural flexibility and capability.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Scope Reminder

This blueprint is intentionally incomplete and provisional.

The demo only needs enough Pathways functionality to communicate the
underlying product idea convincingly.

The visual metaphor, page structure, interaction model, progression
mechanics and degree of gamification remain open design questions.

The research and content provide a rich set of relationships that can be
used where helpful. They should expand the available design
possibilities rather than force the implementation into a predetermined
form.
